Each extension's metadata is a definition: a value holding the name the
metadata carries, the TypedDict its configuration is -- the one
declaration of that JSON -- and plain functions over that TypedDict for
the rules. `zarr_metadata.v3.definition` is the door, and its module
docstring the guide.

Reading a field is three steps, each usable on its own by a caller
holding nothing but JSON. `check(value, SomeTypedDict)`, from
`zarr_metadata.typed_json`, type-checks JSON against a TypedDict and needs
nothing else; `definition.judge(configuration)` is the check, each nested
field's envelope judged, and then the rules; `resolve(field, CodecDefinition, scope)`
reads a whole field in a scope -- its envelope judged, its name related to
a definition, its configuration judged, each nested field read the same
way -- and returns what the scope made of it, `Resolved`, with every
problem located. A name nothing in scope claims is `out_of_scope` and left
unjudged. A field is read as one of the five kinds, with or without type
arguments, and `configuration_of(resolved, GZIP_CODEC)` is its
configuration typed as that definition's TypedDict.

The TypedDict reads as the typing spec defines it -- `total`, `Required`,
`NotRequired`, `closed` and `extra_items` mean what they mean to a type
checker, whether or not its module postpones annotations -- and a
configuration comes back holding what its TypedDict admits and nothing
else: a key a closed TypedDict does not declare is reported, as
`unknown_key`, and left out, so a rule never meets one. A definition
refuses a TypedDict that says nothing of the keys it does not declare,
and a member typed `ZarrV3MetadataFieldJSON`, which would check as plain
JSON; a member holding another metadata field is typed with the field
alias of its kind, `CodecField`.

`gzip`, `bytes`, `crc32c` and the regular chunk grid are defined
(`GZIP_CODEC`, `BYTES_CODEC`, `CRC32C_CODEC`, `REGULAR_CHUNK_GRID`), and
`CORE` and `CORE_AND_EXTENSIONS` are the scopes that hold them. A third
party writes a TypedDict, a rule function and a definition, and extends a
scope with it; a definition checks itself when built, and nothing happens
at class creation.
